How much can this portfolio lose in a bad month?

The model estimates each asset's drift, volatility and correlations from daily yfinance data. It simulates thousands of correlated one-month futures, using a Cholesky factor of the correlation matrix, and reads off Value at Risk and Expected Shortfall. A stress test then pushes correlations toward one, the scenario in which diversification fails.

Methodology
01

Pull the data

yfinance adjusted closes, at least two years, then daily log-returns.

02

Estimate

Mean μ, volatility σ and correlation matrix C for each asset. These are the only inputs.

03

Simulate

L = cholesky(C), ε = Z·Lᵀ. Without L, simulated assets are independent and portfolio risk is badly understated.

04

VaR & ES

Sort the outcomes. VaR is the loss at the 5th percentile, and ES is the average loss beyond it, which captures how bad the tail gets.

05

Stress correlation

Re-run with ρ forced from −0.2 to 0.95 using common random numbers, so only the correlation changes.

What I take from it

—

  • VaR is not a worst case. It is a threshold that will be breached, by design, one month in twenty. ES is the figure to size capital against.
  • The normal assumption understates fat tails. When historical VaR exceeds the Monte Carlo figure, the data has fatter tails than the GBM model assumes.
  • Correlation is the hidden lever. In 2008 and March 2020, cross-asset correlations jumped. The stress table shows the cost of that jump in dollars.
