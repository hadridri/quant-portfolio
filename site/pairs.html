Methodology
01

Cointegration

statsmodels.coint (Engle-Granger) on log prices. Require p < 0.05. A similar-looking chart is not evidence.

02

Hedge ratio

OLS log A = α + β·log B + ε. In the backtest β is re-estimated on a rolling window and lagged one day, which removes look-ahead bias.

03

Z-score

z = (spread − mean₃₀) / std₃₀, the number of standard deviations the spread sits from its recent average.

04

Signals

Long the spread when z < −2 and short it when z > +2. Exit when |z| < 0.5. Stop out when |z| > 3.5, a sign the relationship may have broken.

05

Half-life & costs

An AR(1) / Ornstein-Uhlenbeck fit gives the half-life. Under 1 day is noise, over 30 days lets costs eat the edge. Fills happen at the next close, with fees charged on both legs.

What the presets teach

  • KO / PEP, the textbook pair, is not cointegrated over the last five years. The famous examples rarely survive a test on current data.
  • V / MA and EWA / EWC pass the test, but their half-lives sit at the edge of the window. Statistical significance alone is not enough for an edge.
  • SPY / IVV track the same index, so they are strongly cointegrated with a half-life under one day. That spread is noise and tracking error, and costs consume all of it.
  • The rolling p-value chart shows that cointegration comes and goes over time. It needs re-testing, not a single test at the start.
